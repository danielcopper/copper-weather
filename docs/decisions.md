## Decisions

- D1 (2026-11-18): The app is its own standalone Android app with home-screen widgets and a small app; not a fork of an existing weather app, and not part of the Home Assistant app.
- D2 (2026-11-18): The app reads only from Home Assistant and keeps the last good answer as a local snapshot; the widgets draw from that snapshot.
- D3 (2026-11-18): Home Assistant delivers one sensor made for the app, the app sensor, with a versioned data format: its `schema_version` stays the same for additions and goes up for a field renamed or removed or a change of meaning. The app reads only that sensor, signed in as a normal (non-admin) user. The logic stays in Home Assistant; the app draws.
- D4 (2026-11-18): The first version has one widget whose content follows its size: small shows now and today's high and low; wide adds the next hours; tall adds the next days. A banner shows official warnings, and "Stand …" shows when the data is old. The app signs in and sets the fetch interval. A weather view of its own in the app comes later.
- D5 (2026-11-18): The widgets are RemoteViews, with drawn parts where text views cannot do it, as in copper-calendar; not Glance, which has no custom fonts and no canvas in Glance 1.2. Breezy Weather draws its trend widgets the same way.
- D6 (2026-11-18): Weather icons are animated in the widget and in the app, taken from the dashboard's icons and translated to AnimatedVectorDrawables. In a widget the icon is the indeterminate drawable of a ProgressBar: it runs endlessly while the widget is seen, and the platform stops it when it is not (another home page, an app on top, the screen off; measured on a Pixel 10 Pro with Android 17: 0 frames, 0 CPU). A setting per widget switches it to still images; it is set in Android's widget configuration, each widget set up on its own, as in copper-calendar. While the widget is seen the animation costs about +130 mA for the whole phone; accepted. A frame-by-frame ViewFlipper was tried and dropped, as it looks like a morph. A widget cannot cap its frame rate: `View.setRequestedFrameRate` is neither remotable nor settable by an XML attribute.

- D7 (changed 2026-11-19): Every colour the app shows comes from one place, the palette, the Material colour scheme included. The palette's general roles (`bg`, `surface`, `surfaceRaised`, `border`, `text`, `textMuted`, `accent`, `onAccent`, `warning`) carry the general role names of the shared colour scheme "copper"; the domain colours (the intensity scale, the water steps, `rain` and `sun`) keep the app's own names and later point at the scheme's named colours. Until the scheme's values exist, the palette holds interim values from the dashboard theme the weather card uses. Once the shared colour scheme's Android port ships a ready Material 3 colour scheme, the app takes that scheme instead of its own mapping.
- D8 (2026-11-18): Code shared with copper-calendar (sign-in, Home Assistant client, fetch, snapshot, widget basics) is copied and trimmed. A shared library is considered once both apps show which parts stay the same.
- D9 (2026-11-18): Minimum Android 12 (API 31), target the current API level (37), as copper-calendar: the app runs on phones that shipped with Android 12.
- D10 (2026-11-18): The setup follows copper-calendar: tasks through mise, Gradle dependency verification and lock files, CI on GitHub Actions with Android Lint, ktlint and detekt with warnings as errors, SonarQube Cloud, CodeQL, a check of the PR title and Dependabot. release-please for versions and the changelog follows romm-tender and emu-atlas.
- D11 (2026-11-19): The widget's size buckets, in launcher cells: small from 2 × 2, its least size; wide from 4 cells wide and 2 high; tall from 4 wide and 3 high. A widget 3 cells wide or less stays small however high it is. It is added at 4 × 2, wide.
- D12 (2026-11-19): "Now", the part every size bucket shows: the weather icon, animated unless the widget is set to still images (D6), the temperature, today's high and low, and one line below that is the rain hint while rain falls or is due within the next two hours (the app works the minutes out from the app sensor's rain start), else the condition as a German word. Felt temperature, wind, humidity and UV stay out of the widget.
- D13 (2026-11-19): The hours, shown from the wide size bucket on: a strip of as many of the next hours as fit the width, each with its time, a small still image of its condition (the night form at night), its temperature, and its rain amount when above 0. Only the large icon of "now" can be animated.
- D14 (2026-11-19): The days, shown in the tall size bucket: from tomorrow on, as many days as fit the height, each with its weekday, a still image of its condition in the day form, its high and low, and its rain amount when above 0.
- D15 (2026-11-19): A warning shows as a band above everything while it applies (its start has passed and its end has not; one without a start applies at once, one without an end until the app sensor no longer carries it): the official headline unchanged, in the colour of its level; with several, the highest level and "+N".
- D16 (2026-11-19): While the last successful fetch is older than one hour, the widget says when it was, small: "Stand 14:30".
- D17 (2026-11-19): A tap on the widget opens the dashboard's weather pop-up in the Home Assistant app, as copper-calendar opens its targets; while no one is signed in, it opens the sign-in.
- D18 (2026-11-19): The attribution the app sensor carries is shown on the app's screen, not in the widget, where room is short.
- D19 (2026-11-19): For the prototype, the app is called "Copper Weather" and its widget "Wetter"; the launcher icon stays a placeholder.
- D20 (2026-11-19): The app fetches at an interval set in the app (15, 30 or 60 minutes, 15 by default), and at once after signing in and each time its screen opens, as copper-calendar does; the prototype has no push.

- D21 (changed 2026-11-19): The first version is a prototype, built in five slices: sign-in; fetch and snapshot with a plain text widget; the drawn widget with its three size buckets; the animated icon and its still setting per widget; the warning band and "Stand …". What follows is decided once the prototype has run on the phone.
- D22 (2026-11-19): The app signs in with Home Assistant's own login, OAuth's authorization code with PKCE (S256) and a `state`, each person with their own normal (non-admin) Home Assistant account on their own phone (D3), and keeps the refresh token (D26). The OAuth client is the app's own, small and without a library, copied from copper-calendar (D8); AppAuth-Android was dropped there, as its last release is of December 2021 and it takes redirects only by custom scheme or App Links.
- D23 (2026-11-19): No public page. The app's `client_id` is `http://127.0.0.1:47124/` and its redirect `http://127.0.0.1:47124/callback`: Home Assistant accepts a redirect on the same scheme, host and port as the `client_id` without fetching anything (`indieauth.py` at Core `2026.11.1`, as copper-calendar found). Nothing listens on that port; the login page's WebView catches the redirect before it loads (D25). The port is the app's own, not copper-calendar's 47123, so the two apps stand apart in Home Assistant's list of refresh tokens (owner, 2026-11-19). The app keeps the exact `client_id` string with the refresh token, since a refresh needs the same one.
- D24 (2026-11-19): The Home Assistant address is typed into the app's screen once and kept, also after signing out; nothing of it is in the repository. Only https is taken, since Android refuses cleartext and the app uses one address at home and away; without a scheme https is assumed, and a path, query or fragment after the host is dropped, so the address is the instance's root.
- D25 (2026-11-19): "Mit Home Assistant anmelden" opens Home Assistant's own login page in a WebView on the app's screen, with JavaScript on, since the page is a JavaScript app. The WebView catches the redirect to the app's redirect URI (D23) before it loads, checks its `state` and trades its code for the tokens with the PKCE verifier. When the login page itself does not load, Home Assistant answers it with an HTTP error, or the WebView does not trust its certificate (the load is cancelled, never let through), the screen says "Home Assistant nicht erreichbar – Adresse und Verbindung prüfen"; any other sign-in that does not come about, a redirect with another `state` or without a code, a code Home Assistant does not trade, or a token that cannot be sealed (D26), says "Anmeldung fehlgeschlagen – bitte noch einmal versuchen"; going back from the login page says nothing. The log names the cause, never the address, the code or a token. A sign-in or sign-out under way runs on beyond the screen, which shows it running and its result also when rebuilt meanwhile, and no second one starts while it runs. As in copper-calendar: the cost is that RFC 8252 (section 8.12) says native apps must not sign in in an embedded browser, since the app could read the password, and here the app is our own; a Custom Tab with a listener on the loopback port was dropped there, as no app can bring itself to the front over the Custom Tab.
- D26 (2026-11-19): The refresh token is sealed with AES-256-GCM under a key that stays in the Android Keystore, and kept with the address and the `client_id` in the app's private preferences; androidx.security's EncryptedSharedPreferences is deprecated in favour of the Keystore used directly. The key needs neither user authentication nor an unlocked phone, so a fetch in the background can use it. The access token is never stored. When the token of a new sign-in cannot be sealed, the app asks Home Assistant to end it again, keeps nothing, and the sign-in fails (D25). A stored sign-in that cannot be opened (its token sealed with another key or changed, too short or not Base64, or a field missing) counts as lost, and signing in again is the only way on; a failure of the Keystore or the cipher that may pass does not. Nothing is backed up or moved to a new device.
- D27 (2026-11-19): "Abmelden" asks Home Assistant to end the refresh token (`/auth/revoke`) and forgets it and the `client_id`; the address stays (D24). The sign-in on the phone goes even when Home Assistant does not answer or the stored token cannot be read; the log says so, and that the token then stays valid in Home Assistant until it goes 90 days unused. Once the app fetches (D20), signing out stops the fetches too.
- D28 (2026-11-20): The fetch, as copper-calendar's (its D3, D46, D60): the app fetches at the interval set on its screen while signed in, every 15 minutes (the default and WorkManager's least interval), 30 or 60 (D20); the setting is kept for the app, signing out keeps it, and a change while signed in schedules the periodic fetch anew at it. It fetches too at once after signing in and each time its screen opens while signed in, as expedited work where the system's quota allows; each fetch only with a network connection. WorkManager starts on demand, not with every process the widget's updates start. A fetch asks for an access token with the refresh token and reads the app sensor `sensor.copper_weather` over REST (`GET /api/states/sensor.copper_weather`), as copper-calendar reads its sensor (owner, 2026-11-20); the WebSocket is not used. A failed fetch is not retried on its own; it changes nothing but the last failure the app keeps, its time and its cause, which the screen shows while signed in as "Letzter Abruf fehlgeschlagen um 14:30: …" (with the date on another day), the technical reason only in the log. The causes, as copper-calendar names them: "Home Assistant nicht erreichbar" (no connection, a broken one, or a gateway's 502, 503 or 504), "kein Netz" (no connection or no answer in time while the phone has no network), "Zeitüberschreitung", "Sensor nicht gefunden" (404, or the sensor without `data`), "Anmeldung abgelehnt" (401 or 403 on the states call), "unerwartete Antwort" (any other answer) and "Fehler auf diesem Handy" (the Keystore or the snapshot file); and this app's own for a newer schema version (D30). A fetch that succeeds, a new sign-in and signing out forget it. While signed in the screen's line says when the last fetch was, "Angemeldet, letzter Abruf um 14:30" (with the date on another day), or "Angemeldet, noch kein Abruf"; it names no user, since the app reads nothing but the app sensor. The log names what failed, never a token, the code or the address.
- D29 (2026-11-20): The snapshot (D2) is one JSON file in the app's files, written whole through `AtomicFile`, so a reader finds the old one or the new one: the app sensor's attribute `data` as Home Assistant sent it, a field without a value left absent as it is there, and when it was fetched. A file written but not read back as written counts as not written. Each successful fetch replaces it whole; each new sign-in and signing out delete it (D31). As in copper-calendar (its D45), DataStore was the alternative and was dropped, since it reads asynchronously and a widget's update reads the snapshot at once.
- D30 (2026-11-20): The app reads only the app sensor's schema version 1 (owner, 2026-11-20). Data with another version is not taken: the fetch counts as failed and the last snapshot stays. A newer version says "Home Assistant liefert ein neueres Format – App aktualisieren" as the failure's cause; data without a version, with one that is not a whole number, or with an older one says "unerwartete Antwort".
- D31 (2026-11-20): A fetch, keeping a new sign-in and forgetting a signed-out one share one lock, as in copper-calendar: a sign-out or a new sign-in waits for a fetch that runs, so its snapshot does not outlive the sign-in it was fetched with; a new sign-in deletes the snapshot and the last failure of the one before; without a usable sign-in a fetch stops the periodic fetch while it holds the lock, so it cannot stop the fetches of a sign-in that comes meanwhile. Signing out stops the fetches before and once more after it, so a fetch scheduled while it waits does not outlive it, and deletes the snapshot and the last failure; the fetch interval stays (D28). The sign-in's start and end live in the fetch's class, beside the fetch, so the lock is one class's own.
- D32 (2026-11-20): Besides signed in and signed out, a sign-in can be refused or unreadable, as in copper-calendar (its D45, D46, D52). When Home Assistant refuses the refresh token (400 with `invalid_grant` or `invalid_request`, 403 with `access_denied`), the app forgets the token, keeps the sign-in as refused and keeps the snapshot; its screen says "Home Assistant hat die Anmeldung abgelehnt – bitte neu anmelden". When the stored sign-in cannot be read on this phone (D26), the app keeps it as unreadable, forgets the token and keeps the snapshot; its screen says "Gespeicherte Anmeldung auf diesem Handy nicht lesbar – bitte neu anmelden". Both show the address form to sign in again, and no fetch runs until then. The snapshot's attribution (D18) shows on the screen while there is a snapshot, also beside a refused or unreadable sign-in, since the widget still shows its weather.
- D33 (2026-11-20): Until the drawn widget (D21's third slice), the widget "Wetter" is a plain text stand-in on the palette's `surface` (D7): the temperature in whole degrees, "7°", a half rounded away from zero as the dashboard's number format does, what rounds to zero "0°"; below it the condition as a German word (D34). The texts are the palette's `text`, light or dark with the system. Without weather to show it says "Nicht angemeldet – zum Anmelden tippen" while no one is signed in, or while a refused or unreadable sign-in (D32) left no snapshot, and "Noch keine Daten – zum Öffnen tippen" while signed in before the first snapshot; with a snapshot and a refused or unreadable sign-in it shows the weather and "Neu anmelden" below in the palette's `accent`. A tap opens the app's screen in each case (D17's tap to the dashboard comes with the drawn widget). The app updates the widget after each fetch, whatever its outcome, after signing in and after signing out, as copper-calendar does (its D54) without the update at midnight, since nothing the stand-in shows changes with the time; the launcher has no periodic update to make. The widget picker shows the launcher icon's sun and cloud on the card as its preview.
- D34 (2026-11-20): The widget names a condition with the German word Home Assistant shows for it: `clear-night` "Klare Nacht", `cloudy` "Bewölkt", `exceptional` "Außergewöhnlich", `fog` "Nebel", `hail` "Hagel", `lightning` "Gewitter", `lightning-rainy` "Gewitter, regnerisch", `partlycloudy` "Teilweise bewölkt", `pouring` "Strömend", `rainy` "Regnerisch", `snowy` "Schnee", `snowy-rainy` "Schnee, regnerisch", `sunny` "Sonnig", `windy` "Windig", `windy-variant` "Windig, bewölkt". A condition the app does not know shows nothing.
