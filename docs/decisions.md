## Decisions

- D1 (2026-11-18): The app is its own standalone Android app with home-screen widgets and a small app; not a fork of an existing weather app, and not part of the Home Assistant app.
- D2 (2026-11-18): The app reads only from Home Assistant and keeps the last good answer as a local snapshot; the widgets draw from that snapshot.
- D3 (2026-11-18): Home Assistant delivers one sensor made for the app, the app sensor, with a versioned data format: its `schema_version` stays the same for additions and goes up for a field renamed or removed or a change of meaning. The app reads only that sensor, signed in as a normal (non-admin) user. The logic stays in Home Assistant; the app draws.
- D4 (2026-11-18): The first version has one widget whose content follows its size: small shows now and today's high and low; wide adds the next hours; tall adds the next days. A banner shows official warnings, and "Stand …" shows when the data is old. The app signs in and sets the fetch interval. A weather view of its own in the app comes later.
- D5 (2026-11-18): The widgets are RemoteViews, with drawn parts where text views cannot do it, as in copper-calendar; not Glance, which has no custom fonts and no canvas in Glance 1.2. Breezy Weather draws its trend widgets the same way.
- D6 (2026-11-18): Weather icons are animated in the widget and in the app, taken from the dashboard's icons and translated to AnimatedVectorDrawables. In a widget the icon is the indeterminate drawable of a ProgressBar: it runs endlessly while the widget is seen, and the platform stops it when it is not (another home page, an app on top, the screen off; measured on a Pixel 10 Pro with Android 17: 0 frames, 0 CPU). A setting per widget switches it to still images; it is set in Android's widget configuration, each widget set up on its own, as in copper-calendar. While the widget is seen the animation costs about +130 mA for the whole phone; accepted. A frame-by-frame ViewFlipper was tried and dropped, as it looks like a morph. A widget cannot cap its frame rate: `View.setRequestedFrameRate` is neither remotable nor settable by an XML attribute.

- D25 (changed 2026-11-20): "Mit Home Assistant anmelden" opens Home Assistant's own login page in a WebView on the app's screen, with JavaScript on, since the page is a JavaScript app. The WebView catches the redirect to the app's redirect URI (D23) before it loads, checks its `state` and trades its code for the tokens with the PKCE verifier. When the login page itself does not load, Home Assistant answers it with an HTTP error, or the WebView does not trust its certificate (the load is cancelled, never let through), the screen says "Home Assistant nicht erreichbar – Adresse und Verbindung prüfen"; any other sign-in that does not come about, a redirect with another `state` or without a code, a code Home Assistant does not trade, a token that cannot be sealed (D26), or an earlier snapshot that cannot be deleted (D31), says "Anmeldung fehlgeschlagen – bitte noch einmal versuchen"; going back from the login page says nothing. The log names the cause, never the address, the code or a token. A sign-in or sign-out under way runs on beyond the screen, which shows it running and its result also when rebuilt meanwhile, and no second one starts while it runs. As in copper-calendar: the cost is that RFC 8252 (section 8.12) says native apps must not sign in in an embedded browser, since the app could read the password, and here the app is our own; a Custom Tab with a listener on the loopback port was dropped there, as no app can bring itself to the front over the Custom Tab.
- D26 (2026-11-19): The refresh token is sealed with AES-256-GCM under a key that stays in the Android Keystore, and kept with the address and the `client_id` in the app's private preferences; androidx.security's EncryptedSharedPreferences is deprecated in favour of the Keystore used directly. The key needs neither user authentication nor an unlocked phone, so a fetch in the background can use it. The access token is never stored. When the token of a new sign-in cannot be sealed, the app asks Home Assistant to end it again, keeps nothing, and the sign-in fails (D25). A stored sign-in that cannot be opened (its token sealed with another key or changed, too short or not Base64, or a field missing) counts as lost, and signing in again is the only way on; a failure of the Keystore or the cipher that may pass does not. Nothing is backed up or moved to a new device.
- D27 (2026-11-19): "Abmelden" asks Home Assistant to end the refresh token (`/auth/revoke`) and forgets it and the `client_id`; the address stays (D24). The sign-in on the phone goes even when Home Assistant does not answer or the stored token cannot be read; the log says so, and that the token then stays valid in Home Assistant until it goes 90 days unused. Once the app fetches (D20), signing out stops the fetches too.
- D28 (2026-11-20): The fetch, as copper-calendar's (its D3, D46, D60): the app fetches at the interval set on its screen while signed in, every 15 minutes (the default and WorkManager's least interval), 30 or 60 (D20); the setting is kept for the app, signing out keeps it, and a change while signed in schedules the periodic fetch anew at it. It fetches too at once after signing in and each time its screen opens while signed in, as expedited work where the system's quota allows; each fetch only with a network connection. WorkManager starts on demand, not with every process the widget's updates start: the manifest removes only WorkManager's startup initializer, not every startup initializer, and the notice the unit-test manifest step prints about that removal is expected (owner, 2026-11-20). A fetch asks for an access token with the refresh token and reads the app sensor `sensor.copper_weather` over REST (`GET /api/states/sensor.copper_weather`), as copper-calendar reads its sensor (owner, 2026-11-20); the WebSocket is not used. A failed fetch is not retried on its own; it changes nothing but the last failure the app keeps, its time and its cause, which the screen shows while signed in as "Letzter Abruf fehlgeschlagen um 14:30: …" (with the date on another day), the technical reason only in the log. The causes, as copper-calendar names them: "Home Assistant nicht erreichbar" (no connection, a broken one, or a gateway's 502, 503 or 504), "kein Netz" (no connection or no answer in time while the phone has no network), "Zeitüberschreitung", "Sensor nicht gefunden" (404, or the sensor without `data`), "Anmeldung abgelehnt" (401 or 403 on the states call), "unerwartete Antwort" (any other answer) and "Fehler auf diesem Handy" (the Keystore or the snapshot file); and this app's own for a newer schema version (D30). A fetch that succeeds, a new sign-in and signing out forget it. While signed in the screen's line says when the last successful fetch was, the snapshot's time, "Angemeldet, letzter Abruf um 14:30" (with the date on another day), or "Angemeldet, noch kein Abruf"; it names no user, since the app reads nothing but the app sensor. The log names what failed, never a token, the code or the address.
- D29 (2026-11-20): The snapshot (D2) is one JSON file in the app's files, written whole through `AtomicFile`, so a reader finds the old one or the new one: the app sensor's attribute `data` as Home Assistant sent it, a field without a value left absent as it is there, and when it was fetched. A file written but not read back as written counts as not written. Each successful fetch replaces it whole; each new sign-in and signing out delete it (D31). As in copper-calendar (its D45), DataStore was the alternative and was dropped, since it reads asynchronously and a widget's update reads the snapshot at once.
- D30 (2026-11-20): The app reads only the app sensor's schema version 1 (owner, 2026-11-20). Data with another version is not taken: the fetch counts as failed and the last snapshot stays. A newer version says "Home Assistant liefert ein neueres Format – App aktualisieren" as the failure's cause; data without a version, with one that is not a whole number, or with an older one says "unerwartete Antwort". A stored snapshot is read through the same check, so after an update of the app that reads another version, a snapshot of the old one reads as none until a fetch replaces it.
- D31 (2026-11-20): A fetch, keeping a new sign-in and forgetting a signed-out one share one lock, as in copper-calendar: a sign-out or a new sign-in waits for a fetch that runs, so its snapshot does not outlive the sign-in it was fetched with; a new sign-in deletes the snapshot and the last failure of the one before; without a usable sign-in a fetch stops the periodic fetch while it holds the lock, so it cannot stop the fetches of a sign-in that comes meanwhile. Signing out stops the fetches before and once more after it, so a fetch scheduled while it waits does not outlive it, and deletes the snapshot and the last failure; the fetch interval stays (D28). The second stop and the widget's update run also when the sign-out fails. A snapshot that is still on the phone after its deletion makes the sign-out fail; a new sign-in deletes the snapshot before it keeps anything, so when that fails it keeps nothing, leaves the account as it was and asks Home Assistant to end its token. So a snapshot left behind never shows as a later sign-in's; while no one is signed in the widget and the screen show none of it (D32, D33). The sign-in's start and end live in the fetch's class, beside the fetch, so the lock is one class's own.
- D32 (2026-11-20): Besides signed in and signed out, a sign-in can be refused or unreadable, as in copper-calendar (its D45, D46, D52). When Home Assistant refuses the refresh token (400 with `invalid_grant` or `invalid_request`, 403 with `access_denied`), the app forgets the token, keeps the sign-in as refused and keeps the snapshot; its screen says "Home Assistant hat die Anmeldung abgelehnt – bitte neu anmelden". When the stored sign-in cannot be read on this phone (D26), the app keeps it as unreadable, forgets the token and keeps the snapshot; its screen says "Gespeicherte Anmeldung auf diesem Handy nicht lesbar – bitte neu anmelden". Both show the address form to sign in again, and no fetch runs until then. The snapshot's attribution (D18) shows on the screen while there is a snapshot and someone is signed in, also beside a refused or unreadable sign-in, since the widget still shows its weather; not while signed out, when a snapshot left behind is no one's (D31).

- D35 (2026-11-20): The widget reads its size bucket (D11) from its size in dp, counting cells by Android's sizing guidance ("Determine a size for your widget" on developer.android.com): n × m cells are (73n − 16) × (118m − 16) dp in portrait, and a size up to 2 dp short of that still counts as them, since the guidance's own table, measured on a Pixel 4, gives 337 and 455 dp for 3 and 4 rows where the formula gives 338 and 456. So a widget is wide from 274 dp wide and 218 dp high, tall from 274 dp wide and 336 dp high, and small below 274 dp wide, however high. Both directions count in portrait, the way the phones are held: the guidance's landscape heights, (66m − 15) dp, would make a 4 × 2 widget in portrait, 220 dp high, tall. The guidance's table is measured on a Pixel 4; on a phone with other cells a widget can land in another bucket than its cells say, which the test on the phone checks. The widget is drawn once for each size the launcher reports it at (`OPTION_APPWIDGET_SIZES`, one in portrait and one in landscape on a phone; Android's "exact layouts", one set of views per size in `RemoteViews(Map<SizeF, RemoteViews>)`), and anew when they change; a launcher that reports none gets its least width and greatest height, its size in portrait, and without those either 4 × 2 cells, which the log says. The provider's own sizes (`widget_weather_info.xml`) follow the same guidance as it advises for them: the widths from its portrait sizes and the heights from its landscape ones, (66n − 15) dp, where a cell is least high, each below n cells' size, as its example sets `minResizeWidth` to 56 dp for one cell of 57; this app takes a dp below. So the widget is added at 4 × 2 cells, 275 × 116 dp, and goes down to 2 × 2, 129 × 116 dp. Each part has a fixed size (`dimens.xml`), and from those the widget shows as many hours as fit the width, at least 48 dp each and sharing it evenly, and as many days as fit the height below "now" and the hours, 28 dp each; "Neu anmelden" takes the room of its line. Responsive layouts, one set per bucket at its least size, were the alternative: they show only as many hours and days as fit that least size. The texts are TextViews in the system font, and nothing is drawn into a bitmap, so the system's limit on a widget's bitmaps (copper-calendar's D40) does not apply; copper-calendar's bundled Roboto is in its drawn bitmaps only, which a widget's TextView cannot take.

- D38 (2026-11-20): The weather icons' still images are VectorDrawables generated from the still SVGs of the Home Assistant dashboard's weather icons, which the owner's own generator writes. The SVGs are copied unchanged into `icons/weather/light` and `icons/weather/dark`, and `scripts/weather_icons.py` (`mise run icons`) writes each as `weather_<condition>_<day|night>.xml` into `res/drawable` and `res/drawable-night`, so the launcher takes the mode's own. There is one picture for each condition and form, `sunny` and `clear-night` sharing one as on the dashboard, where the form decides; a condition the app does not know, or none, shows the warning sign of `exceptional`. The script converts what those SVGs use, as its docstring lists it, and stops, naming the file and the element, on anything else; a shape with a fill, a stroke and an opacity of its own is drawn as a layer of its own at that opacity, as SVG composites it. A test fails when the drawables in the repository are not what the script writes from the copies, so the copies, kept in the repository for that test, are their one source. Their colours are the icons' own, as an image's are, not palette roles (D7): ColorLiteralTest leaves out exactly the files named `weather_*.xml` in those two folders, and the test above keeps each such file the script's.
